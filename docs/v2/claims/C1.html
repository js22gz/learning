Tonight · C1

C1 What counts as learned

narrowed 5 attacks: 2 survived, 3 narrowed · serves Q1 · last change 14 September 2026

Encoding has succeeded when the learner can later access the target under cue conditions that match the practice criterion without new presentation of the material — accessibility under that cue set, not mere study exposure or storage-without-access.

In practice (draft)

Count something as learned only when it can be brought back later, without looking at the material again, from the same kind of prompt it will be needed for. Having read it, seen it, or followed it while it was in front of you does not count.

  1. #1 narrowed 14 September 2026 · Roediger & Karpicke 2006 · target: kill-condition

    In Roediger & Karpicke 2006 Experiment 1, the restudy-versus-test contrast after one study episode admits all idea units without an item-level availability or retrieval gate, so “encoded” is operationalized as “was studied / was on the page.”

    Strongest reading and verdict reasoning

    Steelmans to: Kill-condition prong 1 is met for the canonical seed design that motivates C0; C1 cannot stand as if those studies already verified criterion-matched retrieval before the contrast.

    Why: Named design fact — Exp. 1 assigns restudy vs test after one study period with no item-level retrieval gate; initial free recall when given is ~70%. Claim and Bounds rewritten same turn so C1 remains the graph’s stricter accessibility precondition, with an explicit Bound that seed designs often use study-exposure. Brief rewritten. Kill withheld: C1 is still tenable as the precondition C0 needs, once the operational gap is Bounded.

  2. #2 narrowed 14 September 2026 · Tulving & Pearlstone 1966 · target: claim-text

    Tulving & Pearlstone 1966 show many studied items remain available in storage yet inaccessible without cues, so “can later retrieve under criterion-matched conditions” is accessibility, not the same as “available in memory.”

    Strongest reading and verdict reasoning

    Steelmans to: The living sentence conflates storage availability with cue-dependent accessibility; C1 must define success as accessibility under the practice/criterion cue set.

    Why: Named result — cued recall recovers items missed under free recall. Claim rewritten same turn from “available in memory” to accessibility under the practice/criterion cue set; Bounds note availability≠accessibility. Brief rewritten.

  3. #3 survived 14 September 2026 · Karpicke & Blunt 2011 · target: kill-condition

    Karpicke & Blunt 2011 equated post-study learning via nearly identical idea production on initial concept maps (~0.78) and free recall (~0.81) before comparing retrieval practice to elaborative study, so at least one canonical design uses a stricter-than-exposure availability check.

    Strongest reading and verdict reasoning

    Steelmans to: Kill-condition prong 1 is not universal across retrieval-practice designs; some verify post-study production before the critical contrast.

    Why: Genuine kill-condition test: this design equates post-study idea production rather than treating encoding as page exposure alone. Prong 1 does not fire universally. Claim field unchanged; prior Bounds on seed-design exposure gates stand.

  4. #4 survived 14 September 2026 · Rowland 2014 · target: kill-condition

    Rowland 2014 finds no significant testing-versus-restudy effect when initial retrieval success is ≤50% (g ≈ 0.03) but significant effects at higher success, so C0’s contrast does not appear equally when the stricter availability/retrieval test fails.

    Strongest reading and verdict reasoning

    Steelmans to: Kill-condition prong 2 fails — the retrieval-over-restudy advantage shrinks or vanishes when initial retrieval success is low.

    Why: Genuine kill-condition test: meta-analytic moderator shows nonsignificant testing effect at ≤50% initial success. Prong 2 (“appears equally when stricter test fails”) not met. Claim unchanged.
