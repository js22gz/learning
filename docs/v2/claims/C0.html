Tonight · C0

C0 Retrieval beats restudy

narrowed 10 attacks: 5 survived, 5 narrowed · serves Q1 · last change 14 September 2026

After the material has been encoded, practicing retrieval produces more durable retention than spending the same time restudying when the criterion test is delayed, items are correctly retrieved repeatedly in practice with corrective feedback (a single correct retrieval is not enough), and no-feedback multiple-choice practice is excluded. At an immediate (~5 min) test, equal-time restudy can be superior. Once initial learning is equated, the claim does not assert that this advantage grows as final-test delay grows. The claim does not hold when encoding never succeeded, when the practice test is a poor match to what must later be remembered, when practice accuracy is relatively low and no feedback is given, or when “testing” is high-stakes performance rather than practice.

In practice (draft)

Once something has been learned, spend review time recalling it from memory instead of rereading it. Check every answer against the correct one, and keep going until each item has come back correctly more than once.

Don't judge recall practice by a quiz a few minutes later. On an immediate test, rereading for the same time can come out ahead; the advantage of recalling is on a delayed test.

Attack record

survived narrowedone dot per attack, oldest first

  1. #1 narrowed 8 September 2026 · Roediger & Karpicke 2006 · target: claim-text

    When initial learning is equated, the retrieval–restudy retention gap does not grow as final-test delay grows.

    Strongest reading and verdict reasoning

    Steelmans to: The delay-growth conjunct is false as a general rule; a larger late gap is not a reliable property of retrieval practice once encoding success is matched.

    Why: Human re-apply as weaken. The delay-growth conjunct is dropped as a general assertion once initial learning is equated; main retrieval-vs-restudy contrast kept. Roediger & Karpicke 2006 still shows a delay-dependent pattern in their design, but the claim no longer states growth-with-delay as part of C0. Claim field and Bounds rewritten in the same turn; brief rewritten.

  2. #2 survived 8 September 2026 · no named source (attacker's own inference) · target: bound

    Outside lab word-list and short prose-passage paradigms, equal-time restudy yields delayed criterion retention at least as high as retrieval practice when encoding succeeded and practice matches the test.

    Strongest reading and verdict reasoning

    Steelmans to: C0’s main contrast is paradigm-bound; without an explicit transfer limit the claim overreaches educationally complex materials.

    Why: Scope inference only — no identifiable counter-study applied in this turn showing equal or superior delayed restudy outside word-list/prose paradigms under encoding-succeeded and criterion-matched conditions. Did not force a transfer Bound; main-contrast kill-condition not met. Claim field unchanged. (The Bounds field later that day records only the delay-growth withdrawal from ledger line 1 — not this attack.)

  3. #3 survived 8 September 2026 · Dunlosky et al. 2013 · target: bound

    When encoding success is only assumed after a single study pass, a large unencoded item subset remains for which equal-time restudy yields delayed retention at least as high as retrieval practice, so the main contrast does not hold under the claim’s own encoding precondition.

    Strongest reading and verdict reasoning

    Steelmans to: C0’s retrieval advantage is conditional on verified encoding; treating one study exposure as “encoded” lets restudy of unlearned items erase the claimed contrast at delay.

    Why: Evidence vs inference: Dunlosky et al. 2013 rate practice testing high utility and rereading low; they do not report equal-time restudy beating retrieval on verified-encoded items, nor a result that forces a new Bound beyond C0’s existing “encoding never succeeded” carve-out. Operational reading of a carve-out already in the claim; no status-changing named counter-result. Claim field unchanged; no new Bound written.

  4. #4 survived 8 September 2026 · Bjork & Bjork 2020 · target: bound

    When practice-test format or cues mismatch the criterion test, equal-time restudy yields delayed criterion retention at least as high as retrieval practice after encoding succeeded.

    Strongest reading and verdict reasoning

    Steelmans to: Transfer-appropriate processing / desirable-difficulty limits mean a “practice test” that does not match criterion fails to beat restudy at delay; the mismatch carve-out must bind typical surface-form mismatches, not only blatant content mismatch.

    Why: Bjork & Bjork 2020 discuss desirable difficulties and the need for adequate prior knowledge so difficulty is not undesirable; they do not supply a named result that equal-time restudy ≥ retrieval under encoding-succeeded but format/cue-mismatched practice. C0 already carves out poor practice–criterion match. Tightening “surface-form” into Bounds would be inference beyond the cited work. Claim unchanged.

  5. #5 survived 8 September 2026 · Roediger & Karpicke 2006 · target: kill-condition

    When restudy is distributed across the same schedule as retrieval attempts, equal-time restudy yields delayed retention equal or superior to retrieval practice under encoding-succeeded and criterion-matched conditions.

    Strongest reading and verdict reasoning

    Steelmans to: The main contrast is an artifact of comparing retrieval to massed or single-bolus restudy; once restudy is schedule-matched, retrieval does not beat restudy at delay.

    Why: Evidence vs inference: Roediger & Karpicke 2006 contrasts testing with repeated studying of prose passages across retention intervals; it is not a full schedule-matched distributed-restudy design that equates temporal distribution of restudy episodes with retrieval attempts. Whether that equated design removes the advantage is underdetermined from the seed alone. Kill-condition not met. Claim unchanged.

  6. #6 survived 8 September 2026 · Adesope et al. 2017 · target: bound

    For educationally complex materials (multi-step procedures, transfer and application tests), equal-time restudy yields delayed criterion retention at least as high as retrieval practice when encoding succeeded and practice matches the criterion.

    Strongest reading and verdict reasoning

    Steelmans to: The main contrast must be bounded to simpler retention paradigms; educationally complex criterion performance does not show a retrieval-over-restudy advantage under the claim’s own success conditions.

    Why: Adesope et al. 2017 find practice testing beats restudy/rereading overall (g ≈ 0.51) and still show a positive testing effect on transfer outcomes (g ≈ 0.53, k = 11), smaller than pure retention but not restudy ≥ retrieval. The attack’s claim that complex/transfer criteria erase the advantage is not established by that meta; no new Bound written. Distinct force from the earlier vague outside-lab line, but status unchanged.

  7. #7 narrowed 8 September 2026 · Roediger & Karpicke 2006 · target: bound

    At an immediate (~5 min) criterion test after encoding succeeded and practice matched the criterion, equal-time restudy yields superior retention to retrieval practice.

    Strongest reading and verdict reasoning

    Steelmans to: The main contrast is false as an unrestricted claim about “more durable retention”; restudy beats retrieval at immediate test, so the contrast must be bounded to delayed criterion tests (without restoring delay-growth).

    Why: Clerk Apply block 1 (cycle 1/5). Named result in Roediger & Karpicke 2006: after 5 min, repeated studying improved recall relative to repeated testing; delayed tests reversed. Claim and Bounds rewritten in the same turn so durable retention means delayed criterion, not immediate; delay-growth withdrawal unchanged. Brief rewritten.

  8. #8 narrowed 8 September 2026 · Dunlosky et al. 2013 · target: bound

    When accuracy on the practice test is relatively low and no corrective feedback is given, equal-time restudy yields delayed retention at least as high as retrieval practice under encoding-succeeded and criterion-matched conditions.

    Strongest reading and verdict reasoning

    Steelmans to: The living delayed main contrast overclaims; the robust delayed advantage is practice testing with feedback over restudy, not testing alone under low practice success.

    Why: Clerk Apply block 1 (cycle 4/5). Dunlosky et al. 2013: testing alone sometimes fails to beat restudy when practice-test accuracy is relatively low; testing with feedback over restudy is extremely robust. Claim and Bounds rewritten same turn to require adequate practice success and corrective feedback for the delayed contrast. Brief rewritten.

  9. #9 narrowed 8 September 2026 · Butler & Roediger 2008 · target: bound

    Multiple-choice practice without feedback increases delayed intrusions of false alternatives relative to equal-time restudy, so delayed cued-recall accuracy is not superior for retrieval under encoding-succeeded conditions.

    Strongest reading and verdict reasoning

    Steelmans to: Without feedback, MC retrieval practice can harm delayed accuracy via perseveration of wrong alternatives; the delayed main contrast must exclude no-feedback MC practice or require corrective feedback.

    Why: Clerk Apply block 2 (cycle 4/5). Butler & Roediger 2008 (as reported in Dunlosky et al. 2013): MC practice without feedback increased false-alternative intrusions on delayed cued recall; feedback prevented that increase. Claim and Bounds rewritten same turn to exclude no-feedback MC. Brief rewritten.

  10. #10 narrowed 8 September 2026 · Dunlosky et al. 2013 · target: bound

    A single correct retrieval with corrective feedback does not produce superior delayed retention to equal-time restudy under encoding-succeeded and criterion-matched conditions; the delayed advantage requires repeated successful retrievals with feedback.

    Strongest reading and verdict reasoning

    Steelmans to: “Adequate practice-test success” in the living claim is underspecified as one-shot success; named dosage evidence ties durable gains to multiple correct retrievals with feedback, so the main contrast must be Bounded to repeated successful retrieval practice.

    Why: Clerk Apply block 1 (cycle 5/5). Dunlosky et al. 2013 synthesize dosage evidence (e.g. Vaughn & Rawson 2011): final performance rises with multiple correct retrievals in practice vs once. Claim and Bounds rewritten same turn so adequate success means repeated correct retrievals with feedback. Brief rewritten. Block 2 (Adesope mixed retention+transfer) rejected — no ledger line.
